03 / What the line means
This model follows one straight line toward the runway. It crosses the threshold at 50 ft above the threshold elevation, then continues mathematically to the modeled runway-elevation intersection.

That final intersection is not a touchdown prediction. Actual landings involve flare, aircraft geometry, wind, runway slope, airspeed and pilot technique.

For example, with a 3.0° angle, the height change is approximately 318 feet per nautical mile, before adding threshold crossing height.

Data options: online OurAirports open datasets (community-maintained, not an authoritative source); or import the official FAA 28-day NASR airport data for more runway-specific fields. From the FAA APT CSV ZIP, extract APT_BASE.csv, APT_RWY_END.csv, and optionally APT_RWY.csv, then use “Import FAA CSVs” and select them together. Data can be missing or stale. The 3° line does not necessarily equal a charted approach or a PAPI/VASI slope.
